1. DOCTOR'S PERSPECTIVE
================================================================================

THE PROBLEM DOCTORS FACE TODAY
--------------------------------
A doctor opens a patient file. No idea if patient consented to treatment.
No idea if they consented to insurance sharing. No idea if consent was
withdrawn. If they access data without valid consent — they personally
contributed to a hospital-level DPDP violation worth up to Rs. 250 Crore.

Doctors are not lawyers. They should not have to think about consent law
every time they open a patient record. But from May 2027, they have to.

WHAT OUR PRODUCT DOES FOR THE DOCTOR
--------------------------------------
Nothing changes about how they work.

The doctor logs into their existing HMS (Bahmni, eHospital, MocDoc —
whatever they already use). They open a patient record. They see one thing:

   GREEN BADGE  = consent active. Proceed.
   YELLOW BADGE = partial consent. Insurance not covered. Treatment allowed.
   RED BADGE    = no consent. Direct patient to front desk.

That is the entire interface change for a doctor. A coloured badge inside
their existing HMS. No new portal to learn. No new login. No new workflow.

Emergency override: If patient is unconscious, one button. Mandatory reason
field. Always permitted. Always logged. The doctor never gets blocked from
emergency care — DPDP Section 7(b) guarantees this, and our software
guarantees a sub-300ms response that is always "allowed: true" for emergencies.

WHAT DOCTORS GAIN
------------------
- Legal protection: Audit trail proves the doctor checked consent before access.
  If a DPBI investigation happens, the doctor has timestamped evidence they
  acted within the patient's consent scope.
- No liability transfer: The consent capture happened at OPD front desk.
  The doctor's responsibility begins after the green badge appears.
- No workflow friction: Badge is embedded inside their existing HMS screen.
  No tab switching, no separate app, no additional clicks beyond what they
  do today.

DOCTOR'S SINGLE QUOTE SUMMARY
-------------------------------
"I do my job exactly as I always have. But now I have a green light that
tells me I'm legally protected before I touch a patient's data."

2. HOSPITAL'S PERSPECTIVE
================================================================================

THE PROBLEM HOSPITALS FACE TODAY
----------------------------------
DPDP Act 2023 + Rules 2025 make every hospital a "Data Fiduciary."
From May 13, 2027:
  - Paper consent forms are legally invalid.
  - No digital consent + 7-year audit trail = up to Rs. 250 Crore fine per breach.
  - Patients can demand data correction, deletion, withdrawal — and hospitals
    must have a working mechanism to handle this.

65%+ of private hospitals still use paper consent forms.
No HMS vendor has shipped a DPDP-compliant consent module.
Hospital admins know the law is coming. They do not know how to comply.

WHAT OUR PRODUCT DOES FOR THE HOSPITAL
----------------------------------------
One integration. Full DPDP compliance.

Step 1: Hospital provides their HMS webhook URL. We connect.
Step 2: Patient arrives at OPD. HMS fires a webhook to us automatically.
Step 3: Tablet kiosk at front desk shows consent form in patient's language.
Step 4: Patient confirms via OTP. Consent stored in immutable encrypted vault.
Step 5: Every subsequent data access is logged automatically.

The hospital's staff workflow does not change. Front desk still registers
patients the same way. Doctors still use their HMS the same way.

WHAT HOSPITALS GAIN
--------------------
- DPDP compliance without rebuilding their HMS.
- 7-year append-only audit log that proves every consent and every data access.
- DPO dashboard: compliance health score, emergency access review queue,
  breach notification workflow with 72-hour DPBI countdown.
- Bypass detection: nightly report showing doctors who accessed patient data
  without triggering a consent check (the legal exposure list).
- Consent coverage metric: % of registered patients with active consent.
  A measurable compliance KPI for management.
- Breach management: auto-drafted DPBI notification, countdown timer,
  bulk patient SMS notification — all in one workflow.

HOSPITAL DATA IS FULLY ISOLATED
---------------------------------
Hospital A's patient data is cryptographically sealed from Hospital B.
Hospital A cannot see Hospital B's data. Hospital B cannot see Hospital A's.
Not just at application level — enforced at the database layer via PostgreSQL
Row-Level Security and at the cryptographic layer via hospital-specific HMAC keys.

This matters because: hospitals will not sign a DPA (Data Processing Agreement)
with a vendor that mingles their patient data with competitors' patient data.
Our architecture makes commingling mathematically impossible.

COST VS. RISK
--------------
Our software: Rs. X per month (SaaS subscription).
DPDP fine for non-compliance: Up to Rs. 250 Crore per breach.

This is not a software purchase decision. It is an insurance decision.

HOSPITAL'S SINGLE QUOTE SUMMARY
---------------------------------
"We plug it in, our staff works the same way, and we have a signed audit
trail proving DPDP compliance from day one. We don't need to understand
the law — we just need the compliance report the DPO can sign."

3. PATIENT'S PERSPECTIVE
================================================================================

THE PROBLEM PATIENTS FACE TODAY
---------------------------------
A patient visits a hospital. Hands over their name, mobile number, medical
history, insurance details. Signs a paper form they don't read, don't
understand, and don't know how to undo.

They have no idea who sees their data. No way to withdraw consent. No way
to know if their insurance company got their health records without permission.
No way to know if their data was shared with a research institution.

Under DPDP 2023, patients have legal rights they cannot currently exercise
because hospitals have no mechanism to support them.

WHAT OUR PRODUCT DOES FOR THE PATIENT
---------------------------------------
The patient's experience at the front desk changes very slightly:

  "Please confirm your consent on this tablet. An OTP will be sent to
   your mobile. Once confirmed, your treatment can proceed."

OTP arrives. Patient enters it. Takes 60 seconds. Done.

From that point, the patient has:

1. A DIGITAL CONSENT RECORD — specific to this hospital only. The patient
   knows exactly what they consented to: treatment, insurance, research
   (each separately chosen).

2. A PERSONAL PORTAL — accessible at the hospital's branded URL. OTP login.
   No password to remember. Patient can see:
   - What data this hospital holds about them.
   - What consents are active.
   - When their records were accessed, by whom, for what purpose.
   - Full audit log of every data access at this hospital.

3. THE ABILITY TO WITHDRAW — any consent, for any specific purpose,
   independently. Treatment consent stays while insurance consent gets
   withdrawn. Via portal, SMS, or WhatsApp.

4. RIGHTS THEY CAN ACTUALLY EXERCISE:
   - Request data correction.
   - Request data deletion (right to erasure).
   - File a grievance if something feels wrong.
   - Download their full consent history as a PDF.

WHAT THE PATIENT DOES NOT GET (BY DESIGN)
-------------------------------------------
A unified view across hospitals. The patient cannot log into one portal
and see all hospitals where they have data. Each hospital has a separate
portal. This is not a limitation — it is a deliberate legal architecture
that protects the patient's data from being aggregated without their knowledge.

LANGUAGE
---------
Consent form appears in the patient's preferred language. 22 Indian official
languages supported. A patient in rural Maharashtra sees the form in Marathi.
A patient in Tamil Nadu sees it in Tamil. The consent is actually readable —
not a legal document written in English that no one understands.

EMERGENCY PROTECTION
---------------------
If a patient arrives unconscious, they are never denied care. Emergency
access is always granted. The patient is notified when they regain consciousness,
given a full explanation of what was accessed and why, and invited to formally
consent retrospectively. They retain full rights.

PATIENT'S SINGLE QUOTE SUMMARY
--------------------------------
"Finally I know who sees my health records and I can actually say no to
the parts I'm not comfortable with — in my own language."

For an HMS vendor, building this internally diverts 6-12 months of engineering
from their core product. They either delay, ship something incomplete, or
