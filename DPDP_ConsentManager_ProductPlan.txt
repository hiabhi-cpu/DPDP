1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Product Name    : DPDP Consent Manager
Type            : B2B SaaS — Healthcare Compliance Middleware
Target Market   : Small to medium private hospitals (50–300 beds), Mumbai first
Primary Law     : Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 +
                  DPDP Rules 2025 (notified Nov 13, 2025)
Enforcement     : Full compliance mandatory from May 13, 2027
Penalty         : Up to Rs. 250 Crore per breach
Our Role        : Data Processor (consent artifacts only)
Hospital Role   : Data Fiduciary (owns all clinical data)

The DPDP Consent Manager is a middleware layer that sits between a hospital's
existing Hospital Management System (HMS) and their legal obligations under the
DPDP Act 2023. It does NOT replace any existing HMS. It adds a compliance layer
on top without changing the hospital's existing workflows.

Core promise to hospitals:
  "Your staff does exactly what they do today.
   Your patients are DPDP-compliant automatically."

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================

Under the DPDP Act 2023:

  a) Every hospital is a "Data Fiduciary" — legally responsible for how they
     collect, store, and use patient data.

  b) Explicit, informed, purpose-specific DIGITAL consent must be obtained
     from every patient before collecting their personal data.

  c) Paper consent forms are NO LONGER VALID under DPDP Rules 2025.

  d) A Data Protection Officer (DPO) must be appointed.

  e) Any data breach must be reported to the Data Protection Board of India
     (DPBI) within 72 hours.

  f) Patients have the right to access, correct, and delete their data.

  g) Penalties: up to Rs. 200 Crore for failure to notify a breach,
     Rs. 250 Crore for failure to protect data.

Current reality in Indian hospitals (especially small/medium):
  - 65%+ hospitals still use paper consent forms
  - Most HMS vendors have not built DPDP compliance modules
  - No affordable, healthcare-specific consent manager exists for SMB hospitals
  - Most hospital IT teams cannot build or maintain a compliance product
  - DPDP Consent Manager registration only opens November 2026 — first mover wins

Our software solves this by being the DPDP compliance layer that any hospital
can plug into their existing HMS in under 30 days.

4. REGULATORY CONTEXT
================================================================================

Key DPDP Act provisions relevant to our product:

  Section 6   : Valid consent must be free, informed, specific, clear, and
                revocable. Must be requested for each specific purpose separately.

  Section 7(b): Deemed consent for emergency — processing is allowed without
                explicit consent when necessary to protect patient's life or
                health in an emergency. CRITICAL: This is NOT a blanket bypass.
                Every emergency access must be LOGGED with doctor ID, timestamp,
                and clinical reason. Retrospective consent must be obtained once
                patient is stable.

  Section 11  : Patient (Data Principal) has the right to access, correct, and
                erase their personal data. Must be processed within 30 days.

  Section 17  : Data breach must be reported to DPBI. Timeline is specified in
                DPDP Rules (72 hours for breach notification to DPBI).

  Rule 3      : Consent notice must be standalone, clear, plain language,
                specify each data category and purpose.

  Rule 4      : Consent Manager registration framework — opens November 2026.
                Only India-incorporated entities with Rs. 2 Crore net worth qualify.

  Rule 6      : Reasonable security safeguards — AES-256 encryption, MFA,
                role-based access, audit logging (minimum 1 year retention).

Consent notice must include:
  - What data is collected (name, mobile, address, health history, etc.)
  - Why it is collected (treatment, insurance, research — each purpose separate)
  - Who it will be shared with (labs, insurance TPAs, government portals)
  - How patient can withdraw consent
  - How patient can access or correct their data

5. CORE CONCEPTS & DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

Data Fiduciary     : The hospital. They decide WHY and HOW patient data is used.
                     They own the legal liability for patient data.

Data Processor     : Us (Consent Manager). We process consent records on behalf
                     of hospitals. We do NOT process clinical data.

Data Principal     : The patient. The person whose data is being collected.

Consent Artifact   : A cryptographic record proving consent was given.
                     Contains: patient key, hospital ID, purpose, timestamp,
                     SHA-256 hash of all fields. Immutable once written.

Consent Vault      : Our encrypted, append-only database storing consent artifacts.
                     Rows can never be updated or deleted. Only new rows added.

Patient Key        : HMAC-SHA256(patient mobile + system salt). We NEVER store
                     the raw mobile number. The hash is consistent across hospitals
                     so we can recognize returning patients without storing PII.

Audit Log          : Append-only log of every API call — consent checks, data
                     access events, emergency overrides, withdrawals. Legal evidence.

HIP                : Health Information Provider (ABDM term) — hospital that
                     generated records. Relevant in Step 2.

HIU                : Health Information User (ABDM term) — hospital requesting
                     to view another hospital's records. Relevant in Step 2.

DPO                : Data Protection Officer. Each hospital must appoint one.
                     They use our DPO dashboard to manage hospital compliance.

DPBI               : Data Protection Board of India. The government enforcement
                     body. They receive breach reports and investigate violations.

Bypass Event       : When a doctor accesses patient data in HMS without first
                     calling our consent check API. Detected by cross-referencing
                     HMS access logs with our consent check logs.

6. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

HIGH-LEVEL LAYERS:

  [Patient Touchpoints]
    - OPD tablet kiosk (Android app — offline capable)
    - SMS/WhatsApp consent link (for post-visit consent)
    - Patient self-service web portal

  [API Gateway]
    - Rate limiting (per hospital API key)
    - JWT authentication
    - mTLS for HMS webhooks
    - Request/response logging

  [Core Services — Go microservices]
    - consent-service    : capture, check, store consent
    - audit-service      : append-only event logging
    - withdrawal-service : process consent withdrawals
    - emergency-service  : Section 7(b) emergency overrides
    - notification-service: OTP, WhatsApp, SMS, email
    - report-service     : compliance reports, breach reports
    - auth-service       : JWT, API keys, RBAC
    - integration-service: HMS webhook adapters

  [Data Layer]
    - PostgreSQL (consent vault — append-only table with RLS)
    - PostgreSQL (audit log — append-only, RLS blocks UPDATE/DELETE)
    - Redis (session cache, OTP store, job queue)
    - AWS S3 India (consent PDFs)
    - AWS RDS Mumbai (ap-south-1 ONLY — data localization)

  [External Integrations]
    - Hospital HMS (Bahmni, eHospital, Practo, MocDoc — via webhooks/REST)
    - MSG91 / Exotel (SMS OTP)
    - WhatsApp Business API (consent notifications)
    - AWS SES (email notifications)
    - Razorpay (hospital billing)

CRITICAL ARCHITECTURE RULES:
  1. All AWS resources must be in ap-south-1 (Mumbai) — data localization
  2. Every database table must have hospital_id column (multi-tenancy)
  3. Patient mobile is NEVER stored — only HMAC-SHA256 hash
  4. Audit log table: PostgreSQL RLS blocks UPDATE and DELETE at DB level
  5. Consent vault: append-only — no update, no delete ever
  6. All API calls must be HTTPS TLS 1.3 minimum
  7. Emergency access always returns allowed:true — never block emergency

7. DETAILED FLOW — PATIENT AT HOSPITAL A (NEW PATIENT)
================================================================================

Step 1: Patient arrives at OPD
  - Patient walks in, gives name and mobile to front desk
  - Front desk uses their existing HMS normally — zero change in workflow

Step 2: HMS registers patient
  - HMS creates patient record with local ID (e.g., PA-00234)
  - HMS fires webhook to our API:
    POST https://api.consentmanager.in/webhook/patient-registered
    Headers: X-Hospital-API-Key: <hospital_api_key>, X-Signature: HMAC of body
    Body: {
      "patientId": "PA-00234",
      "mobile": "9820XXXXXX",
      "hospitalId": "H-MUMBAI-001",
      "timestamp": "2026-04-12T09:30:00Z"
    }

  NOTE: If HMS does not support webhooks (e.g., older systems), the tablet
  kiosk can accept direct patient mobile input — no webhook needed.

Step 3: Our system checks if patient exists
  - Hash the mobile: patientKey = HMAC_SHA256(mobile + SYSTEM_SALT)
    NOTE: Use a single system-level salt (stored in AWS Secrets Manager),
    NOT a per-hospital salt. This allows cross-hospital patient recognition.
  - Query consent vault: SELECT * FROM patients WHERE key = ? AND deleted = false
  - Result A — New patient: proceed to Step 4
  - Result B — Returning patient (visited another hospital before): proceed to
    Step 4 but pre-fill known language preference, show shorter consent form

Step 4: Tablet kiosk shows DPDP consent form
  - Tablet at OPD desk displays consent form in patient's preferred language
    (English, Hindi, Marathi, Kannada — auto-detected or selectable)
  - Form shows SEPARATE checkboxes for each purpose:
    [ ] Treatment — collect name, contact, medical history for your care
    [ ] Insurance — share relevant records with your TPA/insurer (optional)
    [ ] Research  — use anonymised data for medical research (optional)
  - Form includes:
    - Who will see the data (list of departments, labs, insurers)
    - How to withdraw consent later (SMS to XXXXX or login to portal)
    - Data retention period (as per hospital policy)
  - OTP is sent to patient's mobile for identity verification
  - Patient enters OTP to confirm their identity and consent choices

  NOTE: Patient can consent to treatment ONLY and decline insurance/research.
  Treatment consent is mandatory for receiving care. All others are optional.

Step 5: Patient confirms consent via OTP
  - OTP verified (5-minute expiry, 3-attempt limit)
  - If OTP fails 3 times: flag for front desk to manually verify identity,
    create a manual consent record with front desk staff ID
  - On success: consent artifact is created

Step 6: Consent artifact created and stored
  Consent record written to vault:
  {
    id:           UUID v4
    patient_key:  HMAC_SHA256(mobile + SYSTEM_SALT)
    hospital_id:  "H-MUMBAI-001"
    purposes:     ["treatment", "insurance"]
    language:     "marathi"
    otp_verified: true
    timestamp:    "2026-04-12T09:34:00Z"
    ip_address:   tablet IP (for audit)
    status:       "ACTIVE"
    hash:         SHA256(patient_key + hospital_id + purposes + timestamp)
    version:      1
  }
  - Record is IMMUTABLE. Never updated. New version created on any change.
  - Consent status pushed back to HMS:
    POST https://hms-hospital-a.com/webhooks/consent-status
    Body: { "patientId": "PA-00234", "consentStatus": "ACTIVE", "purposes": [...] }
  - Doctor's HMS screen shows green consent badge next to patient name
  - Audit log entry written: CONSENT_GRANTED event
  - Patient receives SMS confirmation of what they consented to

Step 7: Doctor sees patient (normal HMS flow — nothing changes for doctor)
  - Doctor opens patient record in their HMS
  - HMS calls our consent check API before rendering sensitive data:
    GET /v1/consent/check?patientId=PA-00234&hospitalId=H-MUMBAI-001&purpose=treatment
    Response: { "allowed": true, "purposes": ["treatment"], "expiresAt": null }
  - If allowed: patient data displayed normally in HMS
  - If not allowed: HMS shows consent warning banner (our embedded JS widget)

8. DETAILED FLOW — PATIENT AT HOSPITAL B (RETURNING PATIENT)
================================================================================

IMPORTANT CORRECTION: Two separate consent events are required at Hospital B.
Hospital B is a different Data Fiduciary under DPDP — they need their own
consent to collect THIS patient's data. The ABHA ID (Step 2) is not recreated
but new DPDP consent IS created for Hospital B.

Step 1: Patient arrives at Hospital B OPD
  - Same patient who visited Hospital A. Gives mobile at front desk.

Step 2: Hospital B HMS registers patient, fires webhook
  POST /webhook/patient-registered
  Body: { "patientId": "PB-00891", "mobile": "9820XXXXXX", "hospitalId": "H-MUMBAI-002" }

Step 3: Our system recognises returning patient
  - Same mobile hash found in our vault
  - patient_key matches existing record from Hospital A
  - Consent vault shows ACTIVE consent for Hospital A, no consent for Hospital B

Step 4: New DPDP consent captured for Hospital B
  - Tablet shows shorter form (patient already verified identity once)
  - Hospital B is listed as the Data Fiduciary
  - Patient confirms consent for Hospital B's data collection
  - New consent artifact created:
    { patient_key: SAME hash, hospital_id: "H-MUMBAI-002", status: "ACTIVE", ... }
  - This is a completely separate legal record from Hospital A's consent

Step 5: [STEP 2 ONLY — ABDM record sharing]
  - Once patient has ABHA ID (from Step 2 roadmap), Hospital B can request
    records from Hospital A through ABDM's M2 consent flow.
  - This is a THIRD consent event — separate from both DPDP consents above.
  - Patient approves via PHR app or kiosk: "Allow Hospital B to see
    your OPD records from Hospital A?"
  - This is handled entirely by ABDM — out of scope for Step 1.

9. DETAILED FLOW — DOCTOR ACCESS
================================================================================

Step 1: Doctor logs into their existing HMS
  - No change in HMS login. Doctor uses their normal credentials.

Step 2: Doctor opens a patient record
  - HMS sends a consent check to our API:
    GET /v1/consent/check
    Headers: X-Hospital-API-Key, X-Staff-ID: DR-123
    Query: patientId=PA-00234, hospitalId=H-MUMBAI-001, purpose=treatment
  - Our system checks: active consent exists for this patient + hospital + purpose?
  - Response A (consent exists): { "allowed": true, "consentId": "uuid-xxx" }
    HMS displays patient data. Audit log: DATA_ACCESSED event with doctor ID.
  - Response B (no consent): { "allowed": false, "reason": "no_consent" }
    HMS shows embedded widget warning: "Consent not yet captured for this patient.
    Please ask patient to complete consent at the front desk."
    Audit log: CONSENT_MISSING_ACCESS_ATTEMPT with doctor ID and timestamp.

  NOTE: Our system returns a status. The HMS decides whether to display data.
  For deep integration (embedded widget), our JS snippet visually gates the data.
  For shallow integration (webhook only), the HMS must respect our response.

Step 3: Doctor accesses data — logged
  - Every successful consent check is written to audit log.
  - Doctor ID, patient key (hashed), purpose, timestamp all recorded.
  - This audit trail is what the hospital presents to DPBI if investigated.

Step 4: Bypass detection (background process)
  - Monthly job compares: HMS access events vs. consent check API calls.
  - If HMS reports 847 patient record opens but only 612 consent checks were made:
    → 235 bypass events detected
    → Automated report sent to hospital DPO and admin
    → Logged in our system as BYPASS_DETECTED events

10. DETAILED FLOW — EMERGENCY ACCESS
================================================================================

IMPORTANT CORRECTION TO ORIGINAL PLAN:
The original plan said "first time comer to hospital — no need for OTP
verification." This is INCORRECT and legally dangerous.

Under DPDP Section 7(b), emergency access is permitted as "deemed consent"
but it is NOT a blank bypass. The following rules apply:

  Rule 1: Every emergency access must be LOGGED with doctor ID, reason, and
          timestamp. There is no such thing as uncaptured emergency access.

  Rule 2: "First time patient in emergency" does NOT skip consent entirely.
          Emergency flag + deemed consent record is created immediately.

  Rule 3: After the patient is stabilised, retrospective DPDP consent must
          be obtained AND the patient must be notified what data was accessed.

  Rule 4: Repeated emergency overrides by the same doctor are flagged in
          the DPO dashboard as potential misuse.

Emergency Flow:

Step 1: Patient arrives in emergency
  - Could be unconscious, unable to communicate, or critical condition
  - Doctor or nurse initiates emergency access in HMS or kiosk

Step 2: Emergency override button pressed
  - Doctor must fill mandatory fields (cannot be skipped):
    - Staff ID (auto-populated from HMS login)
    - Emergency reason dropdown:
        [ ] Unconscious / unresponsive
        [ ] Life-threatening condition
        [ ] Unable to obtain consent (minor, incapacitated)
    - Brief clinical note (free text, max 200 chars)

Step 3: Our API is called
  POST /v1/consent/emergency-override
  Body: {
    "patientMobile": "9820XXXXXX" (if known) OR "patientId": "PA-00234",
    "hospitalId": "H-MUMBAI-001",
    "doctorId": "DR-456",
    "reason": "unconscious",
    "clinicalNote": "RTA patient, trauma bay",
    "timestamp": "2026-04-12T03:14:00Z"
  }

  Response: ALWAYS { "allowed": true, "emergencyId": "EMRG-2026-0042" }
  Response time must be < 300ms. We NEVER block emergency access.

Step 4: Emergency consent record created
  {
    type:             "EMERGENCY_OVERRIDE"
    patient_key:      HMAC_SHA256(mobile) if known, else null
    hospital_id:      "H-MUMBAI-001"
    doctor_id:        "DR-456"
    legal_basis:      "DPDP_SECTION_7B"
    reason:           "unconscious"
    clinical_note:    "RTA patient, trauma bay"
    status:           "PENDING_DPO_REVIEW"
    dpo_review_deadline: timestamp + 72 hours
    timestamp:        current time
  }
  Audit log entry: EMERGENCY_ACCESS event

Step 5: DPO review required within 72 hours
  - DPO dashboard shows a red alert: "Emergency access pending review"
  - DPO must mark as: VERIFIED_EMERGENCY or REQUIRES_INVESTIGATION
  - If unreviewed after 72 hours: auto-escalation notification to hospital admin
  - If marked REQUIRES_INVESTIGATION: flagged for possible DPBI reporting

Step 6: Patient retrospective notification (when stable)
  - Once patient is contactable, automated SMS/WhatsApp sent:
    "Your records at [Hospital A] were accessed on [date] under emergency
    conditions by Dr. [name]. Reason: [clinical reason]. For queries contact
    [hospital helpline] or visit [our patient portal]."
  - Patient is invited to formally consent or raise a grievance

Step 7: Formal consent update
  - If patient confirms they consent: emergency record updated to ACTIVE
  - If patient disagrees: DPO is alerted, hospital must address grievance
  - All of this is logged immutably in the audit trail

11. DETAILED FLOW — CONSENT WITHDRAWAL
================================================================================

Patient can withdraw consent at any time. This must work via:
  a) Patient portal (web login)
  b) SMS command to a dedicated number
  c) WhatsApp message
  d) Front desk request at the hospital

Step 1: Patient initiates withdrawal
  - Example: Patient SMS to 9XXXXXXXXX: "WITHDRAW H-MUMBAI-001"
  - Or: Patient logs into portal, clicks "Withdraw" for Hospital A

Step 2: Identity verification
  - OTP sent to patient mobile to verify identity before withdrawal
  - On OTP confirmation: withdrawal processed

Step 3: Withdrawal record written
  - New consent record written (NOT updating old record — append only):
    {
      type:          "WITHDRAWAL"
      patient_key:   same HMAC hash
      hospital_id:   "H-MUMBAI-001"
      purposes:      ["insurance"]  -- can withdraw specific purposes only
      timestamp:     current time
      status:        "WITHDRAWN"
      previous_id:   UUID of original consent record
    }
  - Original consent record remains intact (immutable — legal record of original consent)

Step 4: Propagate to HMS
  - Webhook sent to Hospital A HMS:
    POST /webhooks/consent-status
    Body: { "patientId": "PA-00234", "consentStatus": "WITHDRAWN",
            "purposes": ["insurance"], "effectiveFrom": timestamp }
  - HMS updates patient record to reflect withdrawal
  - Doctor's screen now shows yellow warning: "Insurance consent withdrawn"

Step 5: Confirm to patient
  - SMS confirmation: "Your consent for insurance data sharing at Hospital A
    has been withdrawn. Treatment consent remains active."

IMPORTANT:
  - Withdrawal of insurance consent does NOT affect treatment consent
  - Hospital can no longer share data with TPAs for this patient
  - Existing clinical records are NOT deleted — only future data sharing is stopped
  - Patient can re-consent at any time by visiting front desk or patient portal

12. DETAILED FLOW — DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION
================================================================================

Under DPDP Rules 2025, data breaches must be reported to DPBI.
Our software assists hospitals in meeting the 72-hour notification deadline.

Step 1: Breach detected
  - Could be: our system detecting unusual access patterns, hospital IT
    reporting a breach, or automated monitoring alerts
  - Any hospital admin, DPO, or our system can initiate a breach report

Step 2: Breach triage
  - Hospital DPO logs the breach in our dashboard:
    - Date/time breach detected
    - Nature of breach (unauthorised access / data theft / accidental exposure)
    - Estimated number of patients affected
    - Data categories involved (contact info / health records / financial)
    - Immediate containment actions taken

Step 3: 72-hour countdown starts
  - Our system shows a countdown timer to DPBI notification deadline
  - Automated reminders to DPO at 24h, 48h, 60h marks
  - System auto-generates draft DPBI notification report

Step 4: DPBI notification
  - Our system generates the required breach notification format
  - DPO reviews and submits to DPBI (via DPBI portal — external)
  - Our system logs that notification was sent, to whom, when

Step 5: Patient notification
  - After DPBI notification, affected patients must be notified
  - Our system can send bulk SMS/WhatsApp to affected patient keys
    (patients identified from consent vault — no raw PII needed)

Step 6: Post-breach audit
  - All breach-related actions logged immutably
  - Breach record + DPBI submission + patient notification all linked
  - Available for DPBI investigation if requested

13. USER ROLES & PORTALS
================================================================================

ROLE 1 — HOSPITAL ADMIN
  Access level: Full access to their hospital's data only (multi-tenancy)
  Dashboard features:
  - Total consents captured this month / year
  - Consent coverage % (patients with active consent / total patients)
  - Bypass event count (accesses without consent check)
  - Pending DPO reviews
  - Monthly compliance report (PDF download)
  - Audit trail: search by date, doctor, patient key
  - Breach management: initiate, track, report breaches
  - Staff management: add/remove doctors and staff with access roles

ROLE 2 — HOSPITAL DPO (Data Protection Officer)
  Access level: Same as admin + DPO-specific tools
  NOTE: This is the HOSPITAL'S internal DPO — not a government officer.
  The DPO is appointed by the hospital to manage their DPDP compliance.
  Dashboard features:
  - All admin features above
  - Emergency access review queue (must review within 72 hours)
  - Compliance health score with specific gap items
  - DPBI breach notification workflow
  - Grievance management (patient complaints)
  - Data deletion/correction request management
  - Monthly compliance certificate download (for NABH, insurance audits)

ROLE 3 — DOCTOR / CLINICAL STAFF
  Access level: Read-only consent status for their patients only
  Interface: Embedded widget inside HMS (not a separate login)
  Features:
  - Green badge: consent active for this patient
  - Yellow badge: partial consent (e.g., treatment only, not insurance)
  - Red badge: no consent / consent withdrawn
  - Emergency override button (with mandatory reason fields)
  - Patient consent details popup (what purposes are consented)

ROLE 4 — PATIENT
  Access level: Their own consent records only (cross-hospital view)
  Authentication: Mobile OTP login (no username/password required)
  Portal features:
  - View all hospitals where consent is active
  - View exactly what data each hospital is consented to collect
  - View what purposes are consented (treatment / insurance / research)
  - Withdraw consent per hospital, per purpose independently
  - Download full consent history as PDF
  - Submit correction request (request hospital to fix wrong data)
  - Submit data deletion request (right to erasure)
  - View audit trail: when was your data accessed, by which hospital
  - Grievance submission

  CORRECTION NOTE (from original plan):
  DPO page is NOT for "government officer compliance check." The DPO role
  is an internal hospital appointment. Government oversight is done by DPBI
  via their own portal. Our software supports DPBI investigations by
  generating audit exports on request — we do not give DPBI direct portal access.

ROLE 5 — SUPER ADMIN (Our Internal Team)
  Access level: System-level, no access to hospital patient data
  Features:
  - Hospital onboarding and API key management
  - Billing and subscription management
  - System health monitoring
  - Cross-hospital aggregate statistics (anonymised)

14. API ENDPOINTS
================================================================================

BASE URL: https://api.consentmanager.in/v1
AUTH: Bearer JWT (hospital) or API Key (HMS integrations)

CONSENT ENDPOINTS:
  POST   /webhook/patient-registered       Receive patient registration from HMS
  POST   /consent/capture                  Capture new consent (from kiosk)
  GET    /consent/check                    Gate check before HMS data access
  GET    /consent/:patientKey              Get all consents for a patient (hashed)
  POST   /consent/withdraw                 Process consent withdrawal
  GET    /consent/status/:hospitalId       Get consent stats for hospital

EMERGENCY ENDPOINTS:
  POST   /consent/emergency-override       Initiate emergency access
  GET    /emergency/pending                List pending DPO reviews
  POST   /emergency/:id/review             DPO marks emergency as verified/flagged

OTP ENDPOINTS:
  POST   /otp/send                         Send OTP to patient mobile
  POST   /otp/verify                       Verify OTP, return session token

AUDIT ENDPOINTS:
  GET    /audit/logs                       Paginated audit log (hospital-scoped)
  GET    /audit/bypass-events              Cross-reference report
  GET    /audit/export                     Full audit export (PDF/CSV) for DPBI

PATIENT PORTAL ENDPOINTS:
  POST   /patient/login                    OTP-based patient login
  GET    /patient/consents                 All consents for logged-in patient
  POST   /patient/withdraw                 Patient self-service withdrawal
  POST   /patient/correction-request       Request data correction
  POST   /patient/deletion-request         Request data deletion (right to erasure)

REPORT ENDPOINTS:
  GET    /reports/compliance               Monthly compliance report
  GET    /reports/coverage                 Consent coverage percentage
  POST   /breach/report                    Initiate breach notification workflow

17. SECURITY REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

DATA PROTECTION:
  - AES-256 encryption at rest (AWS RDS + S3 server-side encryption)
  - TLS 1.3 for all data in transit
  - Field-level encryption for patient_key column (extra layer)
  - HMAC-SHA256 for patient identity — raw mobile NEVER stored anywhere

ACCESS CONTROL:
  - JWT RS256 (asymmetric) — each service can verify without sharing secret
  - Role-based access per hospital (multi-tenancy)
  - Rate limiting: per hospital API key, per endpoint
  - mTLS for HMS-to-our-system webhooks

AUDIT & COMPLIANCE:
  - PostgreSQL Row-Level Security blocks UPDATE/DELETE on audit_log table
  - All API calls logged (request ID, actor, timestamp, outcome)
  - Log retention minimum: 7 years (DPDP legal requirement)
  - Automated backup: daily snapshots, 30-day retention, cross-AZ

TESTING:
  - Penetration test required before production launch
  - OWASP Top 10 coverage
  - ABDM security requirements (relevant for Step 2)
  - Tenant isolation tests: verify Hospital A cannot access Hospital B data
    (Run these tests on every release — tenant leakage is existential risk)

INCIDENT RESPONSE:
  - Breach detection: CloudWatch anomaly detection on access patterns
  - Alert pipeline: CloudWatch → SNS → PagerDuty → On-call engineer
  - Runbook: documented steps for breach containment and DPBI notification

19. CORRECTIONS TO ORIGINAL PLAN
================================================================================

This section documents corrections from the original plan.txt to avoid confusion
during development.

CORRECTION 1 — Patient Key / Unique ID
  Original: "our software registers user and creates a unique ID"
  Corrected: We do NOT create a patient ID. The HMS creates the patient ID.
  We create a CONSENT RECORD with a HMAC-SHA256 hashed mobile as the patient key.
  We never store the raw mobile number.

CORRECTION 2 — HMAC Salt Scope
  Original: "HMAC_SHA256(mobile + hospitalSalt)"
  Corrected: Use HMAC_SHA256(mobile + SYSTEM_SALT) with a SINGLE system-level
  salt (stored in AWS Secrets Manager). If you use per-hospital salt, the same
  patient visiting two hospitals gets two different hashes — you cannot
  recognise them as the same person. System salt solves cross-hospital matching.

CORRECTION 3 — Records stored in ABDM
  Original: "records stored in ABDM for record sharing"
  Corrected: Clinical records are NEVER stored in ABDM. Records stay in the
  hospital's HMS. ABDM stores only a reference (care context pointer).
  The actual FHIR data is fetched from the HMS on demand when consent is given.
  This is a Step 2 concern — not relevant for Step 1.

CORRECTION 4 — Hospital B — No new profile
  Original: "no new profile is created in our software as patient is already registered"
  Corrected: No new ABHA ID is created (that is correct). But a NEW DPDP
  consent record IS created for Hospital B. Hospital B is a separate Data
  Fiduciary — they need their own consent to collect this patient's data.
  Our system recognises the same patient (same mobile hash) but creates a
  new consent record scoped to Hospital B.

CORRECTION 5 — Emergency flow for first-time patient
  Original: "If first time comer to hospital no need for OTP verification"
  Corrected: This is legally wrong and creates liability. Under DPDP Section 7(b),
  emergency access is permitted as "deemed consent" but MUST be logged with
  doctor ID, reason, and clinical note. There is no concept of "no need for
  verification" — instead, verification is deferred but EVERY emergency access
  creates an audit record. Doctor ID, reason, and timestamp are mandatory.

CORRECTION 6 — DPO page is not for government officer
  Original: "DPO page — for compliance check from the govt officer"
  Corrected: The DPO (Data Protection Officer) is an internal hospital
  appointee. The government body is DPBI (Data Protection Board of India)
  which operates externally. Our DPO portal is for the hospital's own DPO
  to manage internal compliance. DPBI investigators do not login to our system —
  we provide audit exports when legally requested.

CORRECTION 7 — Doctor access flow
  Original: "data will not be visible" if no consent
  Clarified: Our system returns consent status (allowed: true/false). The HMS
  decides what to display. For deep integrations (embedded widget), our JS
  widget visually shows a warning. For shallow integrations, the HMS must
  respect our API response. We cannot directly block HMS data rendering
  unless the hospital chooses a deep integration model.
